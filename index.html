How it works

1Your file stays putIt's read by DuckDB, a database that runs inside your browser. Nothing is uploaded.
2Messy data is handledTotals rows, notes, other sheets and duplicate lines are found and reported, not silently counted.
3Every chart is a SQL queryOpen "Show the SQL" under any chart to see exactly how its numbers were worked out.
4Click to dig inClick any bar, cell or product and every chart filters to it.

The sample is the public Superstore dataset in its raw form, including 806 junk rows from other sheets of the workbook, so you can see the data check work. The original fixed dashboard is still available.

Drop in the monthly statement from your card processor (the PDF you download from their website), from the US or Canada. Every fee is checked: the statement has to add up to the cent, each interchange line is compared with the card networks' published rates, and every dollar is sorted into who got it. Only the processor's share is negotiable.

Quick check, for any statement

If your statement's layout isn't supported yet, two numbers from its summary still tell you your real rate. Drop it in above and the checker fills these in from its labelled totals for you to confirm.

How it works

1Your statement stays putThe PDF is read by pdf.js inside your browser. Nothing is uploaded or stored.
2It has to add upDaily sales and every fee line must match the statement's own totals to the cent, or no results are shown.
3Checked against published ratesEach interchange line is compared with Visa's, Mastercard's and Amex's published rates for that program.
4Who got each dollarEvery fee is sorted, by written rules you can read, into card-issuing bank, card network or your processor.

The samples are made-up businesses' statements in the same layouts as real ones, with a few problems planted on purpose. US rates are from Visa's April 2026 schedule, the Amex OptBlue pricing guide (Fall 2025) and a published summary of Mastercard's rates; Canadian rates from Visa Canada's own schedules, including the change on 24 October 2026. Each line says which. This is a check, not financial advice.
